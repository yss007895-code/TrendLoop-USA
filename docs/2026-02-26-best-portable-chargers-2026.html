Running out of battery is a 2019 problem. The portable charger market in 2026 offers excellent options at every capacity and price point. Here's what's actually worth carrying.

Capacity vs. Portability: The Core Tradeoff

More milliamp-hours (mAh) means more charges but more weight. A 10,000 mAh bank charges most phones 2-3 times. A 20,000 mAh bank charges 4-5 times but weighs almost twice as much. Match your pick to how often you actually need emergency charging versus how much you want to carry.

Wattage matters equally. A 5W charger will top off your phone overnight. A 65W charger gets an iPhone from 20% to 80% in about 30 minutes. If you travel or work long days away from outlets, wattage is the spec to prioritize.

What the Specs Actually Mean

mAh (milliamp-hours): Total energy storage. A standard iPhone 15 has about 3,877 mAh. A 10,000 mAh bank charges it roughly 2.2 times (efficiency losses account for the gap).

Output wattage: How fast it charges your device. USB-C PD at 20W is enough for fast-charging iPhones. 65W+ handles MacBook Air and other laptops.

Pass-through charging: Whether the bank charges itself and your device simultaneously. Not all banks support this — it's useful if you only have one outlet.

Also worth checking: does the bank include a built-in cable? The Anker 737 does, which is one less thing to carry.

Our everyday recommendation: The Belkin BoostCharge 10K for most people — reliable brand, solid warranty, compact size, works without thinking. Upgrade to the Anker 737 if you travel internationally or work mobile all day. See also: best wireless earbuds to pair with your power setup.

FAQ

Can I bring a 20,000 mAh power bank on a plane?

Yes, but carry-on only. The FAA limits lithium battery packs to 100Wh per battery in checked luggage (none allowed) and allows up to 160Wh in carry-on. Most 20,000 mAh banks fall under 100Wh. Check the specific bank's specs before flying.

Why does my power bank not charge as much as advertised?

All power banks have conversion efficiency losses — typically 10-20%. A 10,000 mAh bank effectively delivers 8,000-9,000 mAh to your device. This is normal and consistent across brands.

Buying Guide: Matching the Right Bank to Your Needs

For daily commuters and light users, a 5,000-10,000 mAh bank at 18-25W is plenty. It fits in a jacket pocket, charges your phone once or twice, and recharges quickly overnight. The Belkin BoostCharge 10K is the standard choice here -- recognized brand, compact design, and reliable performance without overthinking it.

For travel and full-day workers, capacity and wattage both matter. A 20,000 mAh bank with 65W+ output covers phones, tablets, and MacBook Air without needing a separate laptop charger. The INIU 65W at $36 is the best value in this category by a significant margin. The Anker 737 is the premium choice for frequent travelers who need reliability on international trips.

For outdoor use specifically, a solar charger is a useful complement -- not a replacement -- to a traditional power bank. Solar panels charge slowly (most generate 5-15W in full sun) and variably, so they work best as a trickle charge to extend a power bank that is already partially depleted, not as a primary charging method.

Can I bring a 20,000 mAh power bank on a plane?

Yes, but carry-on only. The FAA prohibits lithium batteries in checked luggage and allows up to 160Wh in carry-on. Most 20,000 mAh banks fall around 74Wh, well within the limit. Check the bank's spec sheet for the exact Wh rating before traveling internationally, since some airlines in other countries have stricter limits than the FAA.

Why does my power bank not charge as much as advertised?

Conversion efficiency losses of 10-20% are standard across all power banks. A rated 10,000 mAh bank effectively delivers 8,000-9,000 mAh to your device -- the rest is lost as heat during the conversion process. This is expected behavior and not a quality issue. The efficiency percentage is consistent across brands at similar price points, though premium banks like Anker tend to have slightly better efficiency than budget options.

What wattage do I actually need?

For iPhone fast charging, you need a minimum of 20W output over USB-C. For Android fast charging (30W or more), aim for a bank with 30W+ USB-C PD output. For laptop charging, you need at least 45W -- 65W is better for a MacBook Pro under load. If you only charge phones, 25W is the sweet spot between speed and bank cost. The wattage that leaves the bank and the wattage the device accepts are different -- your phone will fast-charge at whatever rate the slowest component supports, so both the bank and the cable matter.

Is wireless charging from a power bank worth it?

Only for convenience, not for efficiency. Wireless charging from a power bank loses 30-40% more energy than wired charging, which means your bank runs out faster. A 10,000 mAh bank that would charge your phone 2.5 times via cable might only charge it 1.5 times wirelessly. For a standing desk or overnight bedside charging, wireless is fine. For extending battery life during travel or long days out, always use a cable.
