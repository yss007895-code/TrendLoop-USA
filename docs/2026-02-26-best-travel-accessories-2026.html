What Makes a Travel Accessory Worth Buying

Weight is the first filter. Every pound you carry costs energy and, with airlines, sometimes money. The best travel gear is light enough that you forget it's there. The second filter is multi-use: items that serve more than one purpose earn their place in the bag. The third is durability — cheap gear that fails mid-trip is worse than not having it.

What to Pack vs. What to Skip

Pack: A reusable water bottle (fill after security). A power adapter appropriate for your destination. Noise-canceling earbuds. One versatile outer layer that works as a blanket in cold planes. Travel-size laundry soap for 7+ day trips.

Skip: Travel-size everything — buy full-size items at your destination, it's cheaper and lighter. A neck pillow that isn't the Trtl. Travel umbrella (most aren't worth the bag space — buy one on arrival if needed). Travel-sized hair dryer (hotels almost always have one).

The carry-on rule: If it has to go under the seat, you've overpacked. A well-packed carry-on fits in the overhead bin on every flight. Packing cubes are the single biggest contributor to achieving this. See also: portable chargers for travel power and earbuds for long flights.

Buying Guide: What to Look For in a Carry-On

Hardshell vs. softshell is the first decision. Hardshell (polycarbonate or aluminum) protects fragile items better and wipes clean easily. Softshell has some give and usually has external pockets for quick airport access to documents and snacks. For most travelers, hardshell wins on checked bags; softshell works well for carry-ons where quick-access pockets matter more than impact protection.

Spinner wheels (four-wheel) vs. inline wheels (two-wheel): spinners are easier to maneuver in tight airport corridors, but inline wheels hold up better over rough terrain like cobblestones or gravel. For urban travel and airports, spinners. For anywhere that is not smooth tile, inline or recessed spinners are the safer pick.

Size matters more than people realize. A carry-on that is technically within airline limits might still get gate-checked on smaller regional jets. The safest carry-on size for guaranteed overhead bin access is 21-22 inches tall. Always check specific rules for budget carriers like Spirit or Frontier -- they enforce stricter size limits than major airlines and will charge you at the gate if your bag does not fit their sizer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Away luggage worth the price?

For frequent travelers -- more than 6 trips per year -- yes. The lifetime warranty is real, the build quality handles airline abuse, and the built-in battery is genuinely useful for charging at the gate. For occasional travelers or those who always check bags, the Calpak at $80 less is the smarter pick. Away's value compounds over time: you buy it once and the warranty handles the rest.

Do packing cubes actually save space?

Compression packing cubes save real space -- around 25-30% compared to loose packing. Regular non-compression cubes do not save volume but dramatically improve organization and how quickly you can unpack at the hotel. If space is the main constraint, use compression cubes. If organization is the goal, any cube set works. The Amazon Basics 4-piece set is the easiest way to test whether cubes work for your packing style before committing to premium brands like Eagle Creek or Peak Design.

What should I always keep in my personal item instead of the overhead bin?

Your personal item (the bag under the seat) should hold everything you need during the flight itself: medications, electronics, earbuds, travel pillow, a change of clothes for international trips, snacks, and your portable charger. Anything you might need mid-flight belongs under the seat, not the overhead bin. This way you only get up once during boarding and can access everything without disturbing your row for the entire flight.
