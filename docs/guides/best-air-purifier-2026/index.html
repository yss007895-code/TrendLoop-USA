1 CADR Matters More Than Brand Name

CADR stands for Clean Air Delivery Rate -- the cubic feet per minute of filtered air a unit produces. It is the only standardized, independently verified metric that lets you compare purifiers fairly. A $600 Dyson and a $99 Levoit both have CADR ratings. You should check that number against your room size before buying anything else.

The rule of thumb: CADR should be at least 2/3 of the room's square footage. For a 300 sq ft bedroom, you want a CADR of 200+. For an open-plan 600 sq ft living space, aim for CADR 400+. Most brands list this spec in their product details. If they do not -- skip the product entirely. That omission tells you something.

2 True HEPA vs. "HEPA-Type" -- The Difference is Huge

True HEPA filters capture 99.97% of particles 0.3 microns or larger. HEPA-type or HEPA-like filters make no such guarantee. Budget purifiers often use "HEPA-type" terminology to imply performance they do not deliver. If the packaging does not say "True HEPA" or "HEPA H13," assume it does not meet the standard.

HEPA H13 is the medical-grade tier used in hospitals. It captures 99.95% of particles down to 0.1 microns, which includes most viruses. The Levoit Core 300S, Core 600S, and Coway Airmega 400S all use HEPA H13. The Dyson TP09 uses a sealed HEPA H13 combined with a catalytic filter that destroys formaldehyde, a gas no standard filter can remove. Worth knowing if you have new furniture or flooring off-gassing VOCs.

3 Replacement Filter Cost Will Catch You Off Guard

A purifier is not a one-time purchase. HEPA filters last 6-12 months. Carbon pre-filters, which handle odors and VOCs, last 3-4 months. Add those replacement costs to your total cost of ownership before comparing models. Some "budget" purifiers end up costing more annually than premium units with longer-lasting filters.

4 Noise Level at Night is a Deal-Breaker

Most purifiers are marketed with sleep modes, but not all sleep modes are actually quiet. The Levoit Core 300S runs at 22dB in sleep mode -- that is nearly silent, around the level of a whispered conversation from across the room. The Core 600S hits 24dB. The Coway Airmega 400S runs at 22dB on its lowest setting.

The Dyson TP09 is the loudest on this list at 32dB on its lowest auto setting -- still quieter than most fans, but noticeably present in a quiet room. If bedroom placement is your priority and you are a light sleeper, Levoit wins on noise. If you want the best air quality data and are fine with some ambient sound, Dyson is the more capable machine.

5 Smart Features Are Nice, But Not Essential

App control, air quality sensors, and auto modes are genuinely useful -- the Levoit Core 600S and Coway Airmega 400S both adjust fan speed automatically based on detected particle levels. That means you do not have to think about it; it runs harder when you cook dinner and quiets down at night.

The Dyson TP09 takes this further with real-time PM2.5, PM10, NO2, and VOC readings displayed on the machine and in the app. It is the most informative, which is either useful or unnecessary depending on how much you want to monitor your indoor air quality. The Levoit Core 300S has no app or sensor -- it is just an on/off purifier with three fan speeds. That simplicity is fine for bedrooms where you set it and forget it.

6 Ozone Generators Are Not Air Purifiers -- Avoid Them

A significant number of "air purifiers" sold online are actually ozone generators or ionizers that emit ozone as part of their cleaning mechanism. Ozone at elevated indoor concentrations irritates lungs and can worsen asthma. The California Air Resources Board (CARB) maintains a list of certified air cleaning devices -- anything on that list is verified safe.

All four picks in this guide are CARB-certified. When shopping outside this list, check the CARB database before purchasing. Any purifier that claims to "neutralize" odors through "ionic" or "plasma" technology without a HEPA filter is almost certainly producing ozone. Skip it regardless of the price.

7 One Purifier Per Room Works Better Than One Large Unit for the Whole Home

Air purifiers work on the air in the immediate room. They do not push clean air through walls or hallways with any efficiency. A Coway Airmega 400S rated for 1,560 sq ft will effectively clean a large open-plan space, but it will barely affect a closed bedroom three rooms away. For whole-home coverage, you need units in each primary space.

The practical budget-smart approach: one Levoit Core 600S in the living area ($229) and one Levoit Core 300S in the primary bedroom ($99). Total: $328. That covers two high-traffic spaces with quality HEPA H13 filtration more effectively than a single large unit trying to handle the whole home.

FAQ

What CADR rating do I need for a bedroom?

For a standard 150-200 sq ft bedroom, look for a CADR of 100-130. For a larger 300 sq ft master bedroom, aim for 200+. The general rule: CADR should be at least 2/3 of the room's square footage.

How often do I need to replace HEPA filters?

True HEPA filters typically last 6-12 months depending on air quality and how often the unit runs. Carbon pre-filters need replacing every 3-4 months. Budget for replacement costs when comparing models -- it adds $45-70 per year to most units.

Are air purifiers worth it for allergies?

For dust mite, pollen, pet dander, and mold spore allergies, yes -- a True HEPA filter removes all of these. Results typically take a few weeks to notice. Running it continuously in the bedroom makes the biggest difference since that is where you spend the most time breathing ambient air.

Can an air purifier remove cigarette smoke?

A unit with both a HEPA filter and an activated carbon filter can handle cigarette smoke. The HEPA captures smoke particles; the carbon layer handles odor and VOCs. None of the units on this list will fully eliminate heavy smoke in a small enclosed space, but they significantly reduce particle count and odor in moderate conditions.

What is the difference between Levoit Core 300S and 600S?

Room coverage (219 sq ft vs. 635 sq ft) and smart features. The 300S has no app or air quality sensor -- it is a manual three-speed device. The 600S has app control, an air quality sensor, and automatic mode. For bedrooms under 200 sq ft, the 300S is sufficient and costs $130 less.
